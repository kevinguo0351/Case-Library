Case 12: Bagging Co. 
UCLA Anderson Casebook 2016

Business Problem Statement
Bagging Co. makes rolls of grease-proof paper.  The production of this paper is unique in that there are 
many mechanical differences in the production process compared to other paper production lines.  In 
particular, these machines require a larger up-front capital expenditure. Bagging Co. sells this grease-proof 
paper to bag converters.  The bag converters add glue and produce the actual bag of popcorn, and then sell 
it to major packaged foods companies for resale. 
Recently, the R&D department Bagging Co. discovered a means to reduce grease soakage by 10x.  This is 
achievable by a relatively simple modification to the current equipment at a negligible cost.
Should Bagging, Co. make the new paper?
Interviewer Guidance
The objective of this case is for the interviewee to demonstrate and/or think through: 
•
An understanding of the market impact of a new technology and/or product
•
An understanding of industry economies using quantitative analysis 
•
Forming a pricing strategy 
The interviewees initial framework should touch upon:
•
Identification of profitability drivers for the client and the client’s customers
•
Market opportunity and growth
The interviewer should lead/guide the case discussion using the structured question prompts that follow below.

Question 1: Where might the value be in this product?
Possible answers include, but are not limited to:
•
Value for the end-user (popcorn consumers): “cleaner” eating experience, etc.
•
Value for the bag converters: potential cost reduction (will need to be explored), potential for pricing power 
against competitors who do not adopt the new paper
•
Application in new markets (interviewer: you will explore this further later)
Question 2: Where would there be more value – with the end users or bag converters?
This question should prompt the interviewee to openly brainstorm about product valuation.
Possible answers include, but are not limited to:
•
End-user: Unless the end-users are reaching their hands consistently into the bag of popcorn, they might not 
really value the difference in the product – i.e. if they are dumping the bag of popcorn into a bowl and eating it 
from there, they aren’t impacted by grease leakage 
•
Bag converters:  If there is a potential to reduce costs or increase the price per bag, the new product could be 
really valuable
Question 3: Bag converters actually really like the product.  They currently create double-ply 
popcorn bags, but would be able to create single-ply bags using this new paper.  How much does 
grease soakage decrease using a single-ply of the new paper?
•
Interviewees should recall from the prompt that the new paper reduces grease soakage by 10x.  This is if the 
bag is produced as-is, meaning two-ply.
•
The grease soakage of the single-ply bag using the new paper would be decreased by 5x.

Additional Information to be Provided by the Interviewer
Here the interviewer should pause to offer some cost information to the interviewee:
•
Bag converters current cost break-down:
•
$0.30 for two sheets of paper
•
$0.10 for receptor (a film applied to the bag)
•
$0.05 for popcorn
•
$0.05 for other manufacturing costs
•
Client cost break-down:
•
$0.10 per sheet of paper (50% variable costs, 50% fixed costs)
The interviewee should drive the case forward by calculating how much money will be saved by the bag 
converters if they switch to single-ply popcorn bags using the new paper.  If not, interviewer can prompt with a 
question.
Cost Savings Calculation
•
Current cost = $0.50
•
New cost = $0.35
Question 4:  Based on the information provided thus far, what is driving bag converters’ desire 
for this new product? 
Possible answers include, but are not limited to:
•
$0.15 savings in costs per bag
•
By using the new paper for a single-ply bag, bag converters can potentially squeeze out competition by lowering 
prices and still being able to maintain a strong profit margin
Question 5:  What is Bagging Co.’s profit margin without technology?
•
Profit per sheet = $0.15 - $0.10 = $0.05
•
Profit per bag = $0.30 – [2 * $0.10] = $0.10
•
33% profit margin

Question 6:  How should Bagging Co. price a sheet of the new grease-resistant paper?
•
Variable cost per bag = $0.05 (one sheet of paper)
•
Fixed cost per bag = $0.10 (*Fixed cost will remain the same as the original fixed cost of the two-ply bag)
•
Total cost = $0.15
•
Sale price to the bag converter should fall between $0.15 - $0.30
•
$0.15 results in no profit for Bagging, Co. 
•
$0.15 - $0.25 results in less profit per bag vs. the current two-ply bag, offering no advantage to Bagging, 
Co.
•
> $0.30 results in higher overall costs for bag converters, offering no advantage to Bagging, Co.’s 
customers
•
$0.25 - $0.30 results in advantages for both Bagging, Co. and the bag converters 
Question 7:  What are some other potential uses for this new product, new markets, etc.? 
Possible answers include, but are not limited to:
•
Grease-resistant food storage (Tupperware, restaurant to-go boxes, etc.)
•
Auto-mechanics industry (bolts, rings, etc.)
•
Restaurant industry (floor mats, splash boards, etc.)
