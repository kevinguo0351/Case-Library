My hypothesis is that Barracuda’s profit margins are lower because they focus disproportionately on 
smaller offices. This focus hurts Barracuda because they cannot take advantage of economies of scale by 
spreading fixed costs for each office over a large customer base. 
 
To validate this hypothesis, I would want to compare the financial statements of one of Barracuda’s urban 
and rural offices.

Question #2 Solution:  
Barracuda does in fact have many small offices in rural locations. (Hand interviewee Exhibit 2.) Here is a 
financial snapshot comparing results from one of Barracuda’s smaller offices in Chattanooga with a larger 
office in Atlanta. 
 
Does this confirm or change your hypothesis? What can Barracuda do to improve profit margins? 
 
 
This exhibit confirms my hypothesis that Barracuda’s focus on rural areas is causing its lower profitability. 
Even though Barracuda actually earns a higher profit margin on each individual customer in rural areas, 
the fixed costs cannot be spread over as many customers. This results in profit margins of only 12% in 
smaller offices relative to 20% for larger offices. 
 
Barracuda can expand its presence in densely populated areas. To do this, Barracuda can build it’s 
capacity in cities and bid more aggressively on upcoming contracts. The company can also seek to 
differentiate itself in cities more by adding services or increasing the training of its staff. Barracuda can also 
attempt to lower fixed costs in their rural offices. This could potentially be done by centralizing common 
functions into a regional hub.
