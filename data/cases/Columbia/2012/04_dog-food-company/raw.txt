This is a typical new product launch case with a 
focus on market sizing, customer segmentation 
and marketing strategy.  
Case Commentary (Notes to the interviewer) 
Problem Statement Narrative 
•
The product is a take-home kit of cotton swabs.  
Customers use the swabs to take a saliva 
sample, and then send the sample back to the 
company for analysis. 
•
Price of product ~$50 – this is not a high-end 
product.  It’s actually quite affordable. 
•
The company has a patent for this product.  
Let’s assume it’s valid for 10+ years, so there’s 
no threat of competitors. 
Data Set (To be provided upon request) 
The Dog Food Company manufactures dog food that sells in pet stores across the nation.  The company 
has recently developed a new product that allows customers to test their dogs for genetic predisposition to 
various diseases and also reveals the dog’s breeding history.  The client’s CEO needs our help to decide 
how to launch this new product into the US market.

Question #2 Solution (cont.):  
Qualitative conclusions: 
 
•
Obvious: 
There is a decent market for this product that justifies making the investment to manufacture and sell 
the product.  The company needs to make sure that the total annual cost should be less than $375M.  
Since the product is under patent protection, the company can even expand the market by increasing 
awareness through marketing. 
 
•
Bonus Answer: 
This potential revenue stream applies only to the first year.  Each customer is only going to use the 
product one time over the lifetime of a dog.  Therefore, next year’s revenue will depend on how many 
customers buy the product this year as well as how the dog population grows next year.  In the long 
run, the market is going to shrink to the replacement rate – just dogs in the first or second year of life 
who want the product.

Our market size calculation shows that there is a potential market of $375 for the new genetic product.  The 
Dog Food Company should initially target the mass market of dog-owners and leverage its existing 
distribution channel by placing the product in places like pet retail chains. 
Recommendation 
 
•
Dogs will only need this genetic testing product once over a lifetime period, and many owners may 
purchase the product during the initial product launch.  Sales may boom for the first time period, and 
then decline and then stabilize over time. 
•
Although the product has a patent, the company must stay abreast of competing products and substitute 
methods for obtaining genetic information. 
 
Risks 
Develop a marketing campaign to target the mass consumer.  Manufacture the product and get it into the 
distribution chain.
