Pill Pushers 
Case Commentary (Notes to the interviewer) 
Problem Statement Narrative 
Data Set (To be provided upon request) 
Our client, Pill Pushers, sells generic  prescription drugs in the US.  It has been relatively successful over 
the past several years and has products in most of the chain retail pharmacies.  Pill Pushers just received a 
proposed price schedule for various drugs from its Indian manufacturing partner and has asked for our 
recommendation on which 1 or 2 drugs to produce.  How do we arrive at a recommendation? 
-Type of Case: Profitability and Market Entry Case 
-If asked, client’s sales are US only.  Mostly, client 
outsources manufacturing, but keeps everything 
else, like marketing and distribution, in house. 
-If asked, our client focuses on generic prescription 
drugs, so no over-the-table things like aspirin or 
ibuprofen and no patented branded things like 
Viagra or Lipitor.  This means doctors are 
important, either as collaborators or customers (the 
industry considers them customers). 
-Provide an exhibit only when the subject asks for 
information contained in the exhibit. 
-The general flow of the case is to use Exhibit 1 to 
eliminate Dermatology and Cardiology drugs, then 
use Exhibit 2 to eliminate Psychiatry drugs, leaving 
only Oncology drugs.

How should we evaluate the pricing schedule?  
Question #1: How should we evaluate the pricing schedule? (Give Exhibit #1) 
•Trick: One can see in the Answer Key to Exhibit 1 that the trick is to realize that the schedule must be in 
standard units, for example price per pill. 
•Assumption: This case assumes that usage frequencies and volumes are equal across pills.  Really, there’s 
a huge difference in pill usage between chronic diseases and the common cold.  The interviewer can be 
really mean and add this dynamic. 
•Key Facts: An important fact is that profits for all Oncology and Psychiatry drugs > $ 0.10/pill, while profits 
for Dermatology and Cardiology drugs are < $0.10/pill. 
•Take-away: The interviewee should conclude that Oncology and Psychiatry drugs are more profitable than 
Dermatology and Cardiology drugs. 
 
•BONUS: The business insight that not all pills are equal in volume or usage.  Some are taken twice a day 
every day forever.  Others are taken five times for the cold and then never again.

How do features of the Sales Division affect our recommendation? 
Question #2: How do features of the Sales Division affect our recommendation?(Give Exhibit #2) 
•Trick: Capacity utilization of a salesman’s brain comes into play.  A human can only remember so many 
things.  The “Product Per Salesman” row gives a clue. 
•Key Facts: An important fact is that Oncology salesmen’s brains have “room” for 2 more drugs, while 
Psychiatry salesmen’s brains are “full.” 
•Take-away: The interviewee should conclude that Oncology drugs have zero fixed costs, while Psychiatry 
drugs have fixed costs of $2.52mn (42 psychiatry salesmen X $60k training/salesmen = $2.52mn). 
 
•If the interviewee doesn’t hit on salesman brain capacity by looking at Exhibit 2, provide Exhibit 3. 
 
•BONUS: The business insights that not all salesmen are created equal or there may be ways to re-do the 
Sales Division are useful for the Next Steps section of the recommendation. 
•BONUS: figuring out the capacity issue without Exhibit 3.

Conclusion 
 
 
Pill Pushers 
Recommendation 
o Other manufacturers may offer better price schedules. 
o Sales Division’s training could be more efficient, which would increase “capacity” of salesmen’s  
Brains. 
 
Risks 
o Look for manufacturers that offer better price schedules. 
o Look at the Sales training program. 
 
Next Steps 
o We recommend adding Drugs #4 and #8 to the current product mix, because they have the highest 
revenue and lowest cost.  From our analysis, we conclude that Oncology and Psychiatry drugs produce 
higher revenue than Cardiology and Dermatology drugs.  Furthermore, we found that Psychiatry drug 
salesman are at capacity utilization, so adding more drugs requires adding more salesmen, which 
increases fixed costs.  However, Oncology salesmen are below capacity by 2 drugs, so we can add 2 
drugs without increasing fixed costs.
