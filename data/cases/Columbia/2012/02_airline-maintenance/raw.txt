Case Commentary (Notes to the interviewer) 
Problem Statement Narrative 
Data Set (To be provided upon request) 
Our client is a large international airline based in a major hub airport.  For the last few years, our client has 
sold its spare maintenance capacity to other airlines in an ad hoc way.  Our client’s CEO wants to review this 
behavior in a  strategic way to make a strategic decision. 
• Provide an exhibit only when the subject asks for 
information contained in the exhibit. 
 
• The general flow of the case is to use Exhibit 1 to 
compare the client’s P&L units and decide 
Maintenance is one of the better units.  Then use 
Exhibit 2 to compare Maintenance division’s 
departments and conclude something along the 
lines that Engines and Avionics are superior to 
Hydraulics.  A correct answer is any answer that is 
MECE and internally sound. 
•Interviewee can provide MECE list of options for 
strategic decision and evaluate options, or ask for 
info needed for decision 
 
•Can discuss strategic and economic  concepts 
such as competitive tactics, barriers to entry, and 
operational interdependency.  But, lead discussion 
to opportunity cost of resources 
 
• The key learning points for the graphs are 
recognizing ambiguous units (is profit in $ or %?) 
and reading a dual-axis graph

How should we evaluate the Maintenance division? 
Question #1: How should we evaluate the Maintenance division? (Give Exhibit #1) 
•Clarification: the prompt is deliberately open-ended to test the interviewee’s ability to create MECE options. 
A correct answer is any answer that is MECE and internally sound. 
 
•Assumption: Client is focused on between-flight maintenance not less frequent, overhaul maintenance, 
which the CEO feels is beyond firm’s scope.  Do not reveal this assumption until the interviewee asks. 
 
•Take-away: The interviewee could conclude that Maintenance generates a lot of revenue and profit for our 
client, which should rule out the option of halting the “in-sourcing.” 
 
•BONUS: 2 business insights that limited tarmac real estate can be a barrier to entry and that there is a big 
difference between flight check-ups and annual overhauls. 
 
•BONUS: Business is more complicated than chasing the highest marginal return.   The interviewee could 
mention interdependency of divisions or denying competitors’ opportunities.
