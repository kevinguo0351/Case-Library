EdCo 
Example barriers to adoption (continued from previous slide) 
•
Money (funding, price) – The product is likely quite expensive on a per license basis considering the 
entire cost is paid up-front; schools may have difficulty finding or getting the money to invest in the 
product. Solution ideas: 
•
Pricing: Subscription model – Right now they buy the license and it’s good forever, including minor 
updates. Make it a subscription model – additional benefits include a more stable revenue stream, 
and potentially greater revenues per license over the long term. 
•
Pricing: Volume discounts – Provides incentive for schools/districts to buy more licenses, either by 
purchasing the software for a greater percentage of remedial students or by purchasing 
exclusively from EdCo instead of several vendors. 
•
Versioning – Create less functional but cheaper versions to reach schools with tighter funding.  
•
Packages – if schools receive earmarked funding – for example, a certain amount for textbooks, a 
certain amount for software, etc. – consider providing a textbook along with the software (marginal 
cost should be low) to give schools an option to use different funds to make the purchase. 
•
Financing – offer financing packages. 
•
Technology infrastructure – If schools don’t have the installed technology base, they can’t use the 
product. Solutions:  
•
Offer versions that work better on different technology set-ups. iPad / mac / other tablet versions, 
version that allows a third of students to be doing something on the computer while others are 
working on something on paper for schools with fewer computers, offline versions, etc. 
•
Non-market: lobby for schools to fund investments in technology.
