Question #1:  
Question #1 Solution:  
Is the market attractive? 
 
Market size: Interviewee should quickly identify that they need average revenue figures in order to 
estimate market size 
- Average revenue generated by an individual dentists office: $1,200,000 
- Total annual market size: $180 billion 
- With $650 million in sales, Clean Teeth Inc. has less than 1% of the market (650/180,000 = 0.4%) 
 
Market growth: Focus on how you would estimate growth. Case data is 5% 
- Look at drivers of market size to see if market is growing 
- Look at historical numbers of new dental practices 
- Make an assumption about revenue growth 
After they identify these growth drivers, say: “After analyzing these drivers, we have identified that 
dental practice revenue is growing at 5% per year. What does this mean?” 
- Candidate should answer: This implies that at 10% growth, Clean Teeth Inc. is gaining share.  This 
makes sense since their model is to acquire existing practices and make them more efficient. 
(Candidate should come to this conclusion on their own.)

Question #2:  
Question #2 Solution:  
Why is Clean Teeth Inc. growing faster than the market? 
 
Hypothesis 1: “Clean Teeth has a rapid expansion strategy, building new practices” – No! Clean Teeth 
only acquires existing practices and does not build any “de novo” practices 
 
Hypothesis 2: “Customers prefer Clean Teeth Inc to independent dentists”. –  No! When surveyed, 
customers were neutral regarding the quality and service of Clean Teeth and dental practice 
management firms in general 
 
Hypothesis 3: “Some dentists that prefer to sell their practices rather than dealing with administration 
issues (paperwork, HR, marketing, etc.). – Yes, some dentists prefer to focus on dealing with patients 
only and are happy to outsource the administrative tasks to a DPM such as Clean Teeth 
 
 
Follow-up: What segments of dentists are most likely to prefer selling their practices? Older 
dentists that are looking to retire in near-term.  Newly minted dental school grads also are highly likely to 
work at a DPM in order to get experience and avoid the hassle of paperwork.

Question #3 Solution:  
What do we need to believe in order to feel confident that this growth is sustainable? What data would you 
want to gather, and how would you go about gathering this data?  
Prompt if needed: How can we determine what independent dentists think about selling their practices to 
Clean Teeth or a similar company? Survey independent dentists and dentists that have sold their practice to 
Clean Teeth …prompt for questions for each group 
For independent dentists: 
- Example: What is the likelihood that you would consider selling your practice to a dental  practice 
management company? 
- Example:  
Findings: On average 20% of dentists are highly likely or likely to consider selling their practice 
For dentists that have sold to dental practice management companies: 
- Example: How likely are you to recommend Clean Teeth to a colleague? 
- Example: How does Clean Teeth rate compared to other dental practice management companies? 
Findings: 75% of dentists whose practices were acquired by Clean Teeth were “extremely likely” to “very 
likely” to recommend Clean Teeth to a colleague considering selling his/her practice 
Two other competitors competing in the same states had ratings nearly as high as Clean Teeth Inc.
