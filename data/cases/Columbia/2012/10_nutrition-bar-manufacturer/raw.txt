Solution #1: Sample Criteria 
(This is one approach, remember that there are other potential solutions) 
 
Sample Candidate Brainstorming List of Criteria 
Note that this is not a framework. Rather, it is a brainstormed list of ideas to arrive at a small set of countries to 
focus one’s research on, so as to arrive at an answer at the end of the week 
 
1.
GDP to estimate potential size of market 
2.
GDP growth to estimate growth of market 
3.
Overall market attractiveness (i.e. geo-political situation) 
4.
Demographics match the characteristic of target consumer: 
1.
GDP/capita  in the mid-high income range 
2.
Large urban/sub-urban population that works in the corporate world and/or work out: Quick nutrition is 
important to them 
3.
Health-conscious  
4.
Large population of working women (eg. For products like Luna bars) 
5.
Government regulations that may restrict/increase costs of distribution 
1.
How strict is their version of FDA? Will there need to be additional testing and trials to pass? 
2.
How will the product be classified in the country? For example, due to the high nutrition content of the 
bar, some countries will classify this as a drug and can only be distributed in pharmacies 
6.
Cost 
1.
Initial import phase: eg. Transport, tariffs, and distribution costs 
2.
(For consideration) Future manufacturing phase: stability of the government, govt pro-business attitude 
(tax incentives etc), labor and site costs 
7.
Competition 
1.
Existing competitors vs carving out a new market (market structure, product differentiation) 
2.
Competitor response

As long as well-justified, any answer is fine. However, candidate should recognize the following issues: 
•Is there a demand and willingness to pay for the product?  
 
•Looking at GDP/capita relative to the cost of the bar, UK is the only market that can easily afford the 
product regularly. (Think about per day income to spend on a $1 bar) 
•However, in China, the top 5% of the Chinese population might have enough income to afford 
nutrition bars regularly and have the lifestyle to need nutrition bars. Given the higher rate of income 
growth in China, the first mover and early investment might pay off in the long run.  
•Ease of entry: UK is a much more similar market to the US. Consider import tariffs, corruption, navigating a 
new cultural location.
