Question #1
What would you estimate the market size for Specialty Pharma to be?
After general market sizing, provide Exhibit 1 and ask: How many stores does Special Pharma currently 
have?
Question #1
The first part of the question is a classic market sizing. The interviewee can assume that Specialty Pharma 
only serves patients who need mental-health related drugs, or market size the general pharmacy market.
Sample market sizing for mental-health related drugs (any reasonable assumptions are okay – the idea is to 
see a reasonable and structured line of thought):
US Population: ~320M
About 1 out of 5 Americans suffers from mental-health related issues (20%): ~64M potential customers
Only  ~50% of Americans who suffer from mental-health related issues seek treatment; ~20% of those who 
seek mental treatment go to hospitals and the remaining 80% go to community mental health facilities: 
64M*50%*80% = 25.6M
We can assume that all patients who go to community mental health facilities will buy their drugs at the 
pharmacy on premises, so the size of the market is about 25.6M patients.
We can assume the average yearly spend on prescription drugs per patient (out-of-pocket and covered by 
insurance) is about $2,000, bringing us to a market size of 25.6M*$2,000 = $51.2B
Answer to question about Exhibit 1: 440 x .6 x .7= 176 stores currently owned

Question #3
Fixed cost for a pharmacy is 250K per year and marginal revenue per prescription is $25. How many 
prescription per day would need to be filled to break even for each new pharmacy?
Question #3
Simple mental math exercise. If asked – interviewee can be told to disregard variable costs.
Interviewee can use either ~260 work days a year or 360 days a year for pharmacies that are open on 
weekends (but must state that she/he is using 360 assuming the pharmacy is open every day); 
recommended to push interviewee towards 260 works days a year to simplify math.
$250K/260 = 961 (can and should round to 1000 / round 260 to 250)
Need $1000 per day of revenue to break even = 40 prescriptions per day for each new pharmacy to break 
even.
Interviewee MUST comment about this number: what does it mean? A reasonable thought would be that 40 
prescriptions a day is not a lot, and is a data point that makes expansion less risky

Question #4
What is the white space opportunity for expansion? 
Assume 30% of all community health centers are large enough to fit a pharmacy in them.
Question #4
The answer is in exhibit 1:
Total number of health centers: 4000
30% of 4000= 1200
Don’t forget to subtract the number of existing pharmacies:
Existing community health pharmacies: 440
1200-440=760
760 future potential
Interviewee MUST comment about this number: is this a large number? Is there a big opportunity? 
Compared to existing number of pharmacies, it’s reasonable to see this as a large opportunity
