Question #1
Given Exhibit 1: would you recommend moving to 3 months? What else would you need to think about?
Question #1
Solution
Other things to think about:
•
Bigger picture- if you are the pharmacy manager and someone is coming into the store fewer times a 
year to pick up prescription- could have an effect on overall sales (people buy other things when they 
come to get prescription)
•
Addictive drugs – what happens if people loose their pills?
•
Insurance regulations
•
Potentially less work for pharmacists since presumably will see less traffic

Question #2
Given Exhibit 2: Does it make sense to go with 1 month or 3 month? What are some risks to segmenting 
customers or providing a 3 month options all together?
Question #1
Solution
Compare yearly revenue per customer from different options:
Segment A
Monthly: 12*$3=$36;           3 months: 4*$12=$48
Segment B
Monthly: 12*$12.50=$150    3 months: 4*$37.50=$150
Segment C
Monthly: 12*$5=$60
3 months: 4*13=$52
For segment A it makes sense, for segment B it makes no difference in terms of revenue, and for Segment 
C PharmaCo would see a decline in revenue. It would be best to segment, but the gain in revenue from 
Segment A is larger than the decline in revenue from Segment C, so providing an option all together still may 
be profitable. It’s worth noting that average adherence goes up by in the 3 month option, making it even 
more profitable over the long run.

===== PDF p23 =====
Conclusion
• 3 month option substantially increases revenue 
for segment A, and maintains same revenue for 
segment B. Taking into account the improved 
adherence, the 3 month option makes sense 
even for segment B. The gain from Segment A is 
larger than loss from Segment C, so even if 
Pharmaco cannot segment – it is still worth 
moving to the 3 month option. 
Rationale
• Pharmaco should switch to the 3 month option, 
preferably segmenting customers and offering 
Segment A and B 3 month options while keeping 
Segment C on the yearly option. 
Recommendation
• Estimate loss from other sales to make sure it 
does not overshadow gain in prescription 
revenue
• Check the 3 month option works with insurance 
regulation
• Try pilot with specific drugs
Next Steps
• Need to take into account loss from other sales 
because patients come less to the pharmacy in 3 
month option
• 3 month option may contradict insurance 
regulations
• May lose customers who prefer 1 month option 
and can get the 1 month option from competitors
