Electro-Growth
You have been hired by the head of business development at Electro-Growth, a large electronics 
manufacturer in the U.S. The company is interested in increasing its revenue by manufacturing new 
products. Currently, your division develops three kinds of products:
1.
Innovative Products – Brand new products that represent 10% of all products your division produces. 
Each Innovative Product developed is expected to generate $40M in new revenue.
2.
New Versions of Products – New versions of existing products that represent 40% of all products your 
division produces. Each New Version product is expected to generate $10M in new revenue.
3.
Current Products – Current products represent 50% of all products your division produces and does not 
generate new revenue; it only guarantees consistent revenue from these products.
You are contemplating restructuring your division to improve your capacity to produce new products. 
Currently, it takes your division 24 months to develop an Innovative Product and 12 months to develop a 
New Version of a product. After the restructuring it will take you 18 months to develop both products.
Should you go ahead with restructuring?

Electro-Growth
Sample Framework
The interviewee can discuss different issues that 
arise from restructuring, such as:
1. Potential Gains From Restructuring
-
More revenue from increased manufacturing of 
innovative products
-
Improvement in company brand from having 
more innovative products
2. Potential Restructuring Costs / Difficulties
-
HR, R&D, and manufacturing costs of 
restructuring (interviewee is then told 
restructuring has no costs)
-
Loss of revenue from existing products due to 
deprioritization
-
Competitive attack from other electronics 
manufacturers developing new products more 
fiercely
As mentioned, the main issue is not the framework, 
but the calculation of the revenue. Interviewer 
should direct the interviewee to calculating gain / 
loss from restructuring, regardless of the 
framework.
Sample Framework
The main issue here is understanding how to set-
up the math and completing it correctly: there is 
one right answer. The interviewer should not focus 
on the framework too much and instead push the 
candidate towards doing the math. 
Top candidates will realize that the math can be 
simplified by assuming the company produces 10 
total products: 1 (10%) innovative, 4 (40%) new 
version, and 5 (50%) current.
Guidance for Interviewer
•
There is no cost for restructuring and no 
change in the cost structure after restructuring
•
There is no change in the relative quantities of 
each type of product (innovative still represent 
10%, new version still represent 40%, current 
still represent 50%)
•
The only goal is to increase revenue from the 
development of new products

Electro-Growth
Conclusion
• Restructuring as planned will cause annual 
revenue to drop from $60M to $54M
Rationale
• Electro-Growth should not move forward with 
restructuring
Recommendation
• Devise new plan for increasing revenue 
• Look into increasing the number of innovative 
products developed, as they generate 
substantially more revenue
• Continue researching new ways to decrease the 
time needed to develop products
Next Steps
• Cancelling restructuring could cause a stagnation 
in growth
• Competitors may be pushing forward more 
innovative products
