Case 5: Score Music 
Our client is Score Music, a major recorded music company which signs, records, distributes and markets a variety of musical acts in the 
United States. They have hired us to make recommendations about how they can increase their revenues. 
This is a complete, end-to-end interviewer-led case about the music industry. The interviewer should immediately follow the case prompt with the 
first question. The candidate can take about a minute to formulate his/her responses to questions, particularly those that will require framework 
creation. This case is lengthy and the interviewer will need to determine if some sections should be skipped if the candidate is taking too long to 
answer questions. As always, push the candidate to synthesize key insights after calculations or chart interpretation. The market sizing associated 
with Question #3 can take many different forms. The interviewer should look for the candidate to be as structured as possible and generate 
reasonable assumptions throughout the exercise.  
 
A strong candidate will touch on all of the categories listed in Questions #1 and #5. Note that it is not important to touch upon all of the sub-
bullet points in these questions, given the time constraints of a case interview. A strong candidate will recognize that piracy is a strong force 
within the music industry and bring that point into the discussion wherever appropriate. This case is meant to test the candidate’s ability to 
brainstorm different avenues of investigation for each of the questions. Superior candidates will find ways to apply structure to their 
brainstorming and will be able to connect the different parts of the case together to draw overall lessons. If you have time you can test this at the 
end of Question #6 by asking the candidate to summarize what they have learned from their analysis.

• Separate the population into age groups that have different music purchasing habits (column 1). 
• Estimate the % of the US population represented by these groups (column 2). 
• Multiply the %s in column 2 by 300 (the US population in millions) to determine the total size of the group (column 3). 
• Estimate the average # of albums (column 4) and tracks (column 5) a person in each group purchases per year. 
• Recognize that albums, whether physical or digital, generally sell for around $10 and digital tracks sell for around $1. 
• Multiply column 4 by $10 and column 5 by $1 and add these figures together to reach the average revenue generated by individuals in 
each group (column 6). 
• Take the total population of each group (column 3) and multiply by the average revenue per person (column 6) to find the total revenue 
for each group (column 7). 
• Add the total revenue for each group (column 7) to find the total yearly revenue in the US for recorded music sales.
