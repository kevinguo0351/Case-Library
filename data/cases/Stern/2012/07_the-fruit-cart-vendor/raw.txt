Case 7: Fruit Cart Vendor 
You find out you have a long-lost uncle, and he is a fruit cart vendor at a street corner in downtown Boston. Your uncle knows that you are a 
business school student, and he thinks you might be able to help him increase the profitability of his business. He wants to increase his profit 
during this year by 10%, and he will plow these profits back into the business. He intends to achieve this by expanding into selling 
vegetables.   
   
He wants your help to determine if he can reach his goal. 
This is a good first case for a candidate to take as it is structurally simple and requires little more than a basic profitability analysis. The 
candidate should spend most of their time exploring all the revenue sources and costs associated with entering the vegetable market. They should 
calculate the expected profit after the introduction of vegetables and make a recommendation based on their calculations. They should also 
include other considerations such as competitor response, changes in customer type, the uncle’s knowledge of vegetables, etc. 
 
Much of the background information is not directly relevant to the core of the case, and candidates should retain focus and ignore extraneous 
information. Expected demand has already been estimated and all the other figures for the profitability calculation are contained in the case. A 
candidate may justifiably consider the reliability of those estimates and the overall viability of the business, but these extra considerations should 
be in addition to, not instead of, the core analysis.

in any knock-on effect on fruit sales. Finally, calculate the net overall change in profit and compare to the 10%/$1,000 target.

cancel out and move on without doing unnecessary calculations.

•
At current estimated demand levels, your uncle would be able to surpass his profit target 
of $1,000 by $100 next year.   
•
The candidate should also make at least one recommendation for further study or 
analysis, as there are many grey areas in the case. For example, your uncle may want to 
do additional surveys of his customers to determine which vegetables would be most 
popular, which could increase daily demand further.  
•
Since he will just make his target by $100, he must be confident that his lack of expertise 
with vegetables will not affect his ability to sell vegetables, or that this is factored into 
the estimates. He must also be aware that his lack of expertise may lead to spoilage 
rates that could eat into his profits to a degree that he did not anticipate. 
•
It is also important that he is comfortable that he can find the additional cash flow for the 
investment (at least $200 to finance the additional permit costs for carrying vegetables).
