Case 8: A Sticky Paper Situation 
Your client is PaperCo, a manufacturer of specialty papers which are sold to commercial printers in the US.  PaperCo produces self-adhesive 
sheeted papers that are ultimately used in a variety of labeling applications – often, eventually, to service CPG firms and for billboards. 
PaperCo’s operations are profitable, but the business has failed to grow over the past few years.  The client would like to invest in the 
business to restore growth and you have been asked to identify opportunities. 
For most candidates this case will be primarily focused on reaching and working through the math, leaving little time for high-level strategy. The 
difficulty of this case lies in feeling comfortable with the relatively obscure product/industry and in obtaining the right information to make the 
necessary profitability and ROI (return on investment) calculations. There are some key pieces of information which the candidate will need to 
obtain in order to head in the right direction. If they struggle to ask the right questions to get that information then you can guide them to the 
right place. Your choice about how strict to be in giving up this information will make a big difference to the difficulty of the case. 
 
The candidate may choose to clarify objectives upfront (this would be wise on his/her part). If so, to make the case easier you can specify that the 
primary objective is ROI, or to test the candidate you can ask them to choose a suitable objective instead, or simply say that it is not specified, 
encouraging them to use their judgment. 
 
For the ROI calculation the candidate will be comparing a fixed cost to an annual return. They therefore need to choose a time horizon. If asked, 
tell them to calculate ROI over 1 year.

• The client should expand production in the medium size printer segment, as this generates 
the largest ROI over the client’s preferred time horizon (1-year); the ROI available is very 
attractive from an investment standpoint 
• Additionally, the smaller cost of expansion in the medium printer segment means a reduced 
financing need and a resulting diminished risk from the investment 
• The client can also consider expansion into the large size printer segment at a later date 
(depending on capacity) 
• If the client considers a longer time horizon for its ROI calculation the large printer segment 
may become more attractive 
• These recommendations are based on no growth on the markets, expansion in one or the 
other could tip the balance 
• There is no information regarding competitive response, but given the aggressive
