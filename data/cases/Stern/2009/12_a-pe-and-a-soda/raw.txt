12. A PE and a Soda              _________________  
Type of Case: Profitability/ Exit strategy  
Difficulty: High  
   
Opening  
   
Your client is a private equity firm that has purchased a plastic materials manufacturer that 
makes products such as plastic soda bottles. The client is looking to cash out of their investment 
within 5 years and are looking to you help them improve the profitability of the manufacturer.  
   
Analysis  
Products  
Split into bottles and non-bottling goods. The bottles are delivered locally to the clients, large 
bottlers such as Coke or Pepsi. Non-bottling products (such as advertising displays for stores) are 
delivered directly to the establishments that will use them, such as gas stations. Bottles are made 
to spec whereas the non-bottling goods are not.  
   
Manufacturing  
The company has two plants:  
•       1 in the southeast US that manufactures only bottles with capacity of 1.5 billion 
bottles  
•       1 in Canada that produces bottles and non-bottling materials with a capacity of 500 
million bottles  
Assumptions:  
•       No downtime, plants are fully utilized  
•       Plants are using new, highly efficient machinery  
   
The Canadian plant can convert the non-bottling production to bottling to add 500M in capacity 
to bottle production. Cost of conversion is $3 M.  
•       After exploring costs, revenues, and margins the respondent should assess whether 
or not to convert the Canadian plant to bottles only. They should calculate additional 
profit after conversion to determine if it is worth the $3 M conversion cost:  
o      Current profit from non-bottling in Canada: $2 M  
o      Projected profit from additional bottling capacity in Canada:  
.5 B bottles * $.015 margin per bottle = $7.5 M  
o      Conversion adds $5.5 M in gross profit annually – breakeven in 1st year  
       If asked about financing, the firm has sufficient cash on hand to fund 
the conversion and respondent does not need to discount the cash flows.  
o      Assume no downtime for conversion  
   
Costs / Revenues  
Revenue  
•       Bottles sell for $.05 each and utilization is currently at 100% for the factories  
•       Non-Bottling generates $20M in revenue

Costs  
•       For each bottle: $.02 for plastic, $.01 for machinery, and $.005 for labor  
o      Respondent should be prompted to provide potential variable costs  
•       Non-bottling has total variable costs of $18M  
•       SG&A across entire firm is $20 M  
  Margin calculation (respondent should calculate)  
•       Bottles have 30% margin as they make $.015 per bottle  
o      (Price – Variable Costs) / Price = ($.05 - $.035) / $.05 = .3 = 30%  
•       Bottling generates 10% margins  
o      ($20M - $18M) / $20M = .1 = 10%  
   
Market / Sales  
•       Bottling market growing at 10% a year in North America; for purposes of the case 
assume both plants can service entire market  
•       Firm’s sales been growing at 15% per year, projected 15% growth for next year as 
well  
•       Company has 30% share of bottling market  
•       Firm has higher quality products and manufactures at lower costs due to more 
efficient machinery  
•       Respondent should calculate total market and projected market expansion next year 
given the 10% growth. Should use this with projected firm sales growth to assess 
feasibility of selling additional volume produced by expanding production capacity 
through Canadian plant conversion.  
o      Using market share and production levels, calculate market size:  
30% * market = 2 B bottles; market = 6.67 B bottles  
o      Projected market growth = 6.67 B * 10% = .67 B  
o      Firm’s projected sales = current sales + 15% growth = 2 B * 1.15 = 2.3 B 
bottles  
o      Firm’s sales growing by .3 B bottles and market expanding by .67 B;  
       Respondent should comment on feasibility  
       Respondent could support feasibility argument by looking at 
required market share to continue sales growth: 
2.3 / 6.67 = 34% or roughly 1/3 of market instead of current 30%  
   
Recommendations  
The manufacturer should convert the non-bottling manufacturing in Canada to bottling as it 
offers better margins, assuming they can maintain sales growth. The additional capacity is 
needed to match next year’s anticipated demand. The firm will still run out of capacity the year 
after, so one consideration would be adding another plant or expanding existing plants to extend 
production capacity to keep pace with the market growth. Expansion plans should fit within the 
PE firm’s goal of exiting within 5 years.  
Other considerations:  
•       Negotiate with suppliers / customers  
•       Increase prices due to higher quality products
