18. Want a Beer?                                        _________________  
Type of Case: Supply Chain/ Operations  
Difficulty: High 
FEMSA is interested in selling its beer business to Heinekin or SAB Miller. FEMSA is based 
mostly in Mexico. Don’t focus on the price of the transaction, but focus on the strategic view of 
the case. FEMSA is consumer goods centric with three major units: Beer $3B. Has 7 – 8 brands 
and 75% of revenue is produced in Mexico. Other 25% is US. They have famous brands such as 
Dos Equis, Tecate, and Sol. They’ve seen 3% - 5% growth, which is in line with their mature 
market. Biggest competitor is modelo who has been more profitable. Modelo (mfg of Corona) 
has a 55% share and FEMSA has 45%. 50% of rev comes from Corona. FEMSA has 20% 
operating margins. Although it is a duopoly, they have tried to avoid price wars, so they are 
cooperating to a degree, but not a ton.  
The second division si CocaCola FEMSA. This is bottling facility for Coca Cola. It’s the largest 
in Latin America and second largest in the World. $7 B business. Fastest growing business 
within FEMSA. Made lots of acquisitions and have had strong organic growth. Highly profitable.  
Third business is FEMSA retail ($4B). Chain of convenience stores similar to 7-11. Most 
ddominate player in the market. Has very fast growth – 30%. Typical of the retail world, margins 
are slim. They’ve managed 6% margins, which are above average.  
In the last 10 – 15 years, lots of mergers and acquisitions in the beer industry. The three major 
players are SAB Miller, Imbev, and Heinekin. Local brands tend to dominate in local markets. 
Strong emotional aspect to local brands, but most brands are moving toward a global reach. 
Typically this is because there’s sourcing, mfg, and distribution synergies that help. A tthis point 
you are free to choose the side of FEMSA or Heinekin. Let me know what you choose and what 
you would advise the client.  
-Chose FEMSA, based only on the fact that he had tons of info regarding FEMSA.  
-Asked whether FEMSA is public or private. Publicly traded. That changes the equation and how 
the CEO can approach this case.  
Five buckets: share price, competition, strategic, operational, investment options.  
Diversification premium. Transaction price. How the transaction would change the industry’s 
beta and company’s beta.  
Within Company, talked about Modelo being a faster growing company, being squeezed out of 
industry, and new entrants coming in.  
Withing strategy, talked about duopoloy currently in place and possible expansion plans.

Within operational, more value in a large brand. Any synergies that could be sold. Any profit 
sharing or results sharing opportunities.  
Investment options – When the money does come in, what to do with that money. Dividends, 
another acquisition, grow current businesses, improve current operations.  
Diversification premium: when you’re in 3 different business, you’re dinged in share price. The 
value of the firm is worth more separately. Transaction itself: CEO needs to know what the right 
price is. Don’t get into numbers, but talk about it.  
Once you get out of the beer business, no longer in the same industry as a company. FEMSA was 
originally a consumer goods company. Now more a distribution company. The profile of the 
company changes. There will be a change in shareholder. Will also change the beta. As a CEO, 
need to understand these issues. Institutions that would previously fit in their beverage portfolio, 
no longer fit. Getting squeezed out of industry. We’re growing slower than our competitor. 
Given the trends, we will be squeezed out. If a third competitor comes into the equation, 
coopetition becomes harder to maintain and the company could be squeezed out further.  
   
Based on all these considerations the final recommendation is to sell the division.
