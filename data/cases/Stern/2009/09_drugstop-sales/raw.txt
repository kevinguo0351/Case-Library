9. DrugStop Sales                                   _________________  
Type of Case: Product Mix Analysis  
Difficulty: Moderate  
   
Opening  
Our client is the CEO of DrugStop Co., a large pharmacy/drugstore operating stores across North 
America. The retailer had been enjoying growing sales over the past 12 months, significantly 
increasing its market share over the period. Recently, however, the CEO has become 
increasingly concerned about a declining trend in overall profit. He has approached our firm in 
order to help him determine the reasons for the decline as well as recommendations on 
appropriate actions.  
   
Background (Provide the following information if requested by interviewee)  
•       The overall market has also experienced stable growth, although at a slower pace  
•       Market share has grown at a steady rate  
•       There have been no significant changes in the competitive landscape over the past 
12 months (marketing, new products/services, new entrants, substitutes, etc.)  
•       DrugStop’s prices have not changed over the past 12 months  
•       DrugStop’s sales quantities have increased over the past 12 months  
•       DrugStop’s costs have not changed over the past 12 months  
•       Demand, income and consumer needs have not changed over the past 12 months  
•       DrugStop has introduced a whole series of complementary convenience products 
and generic drugs over the past six months  
   
Areas of Discussion  
•       The drugstore market:  
o      Competitors:  
       Sales  
       Market share  
       Substitutes  
o      Consumers:  
       Changes in demand  
       Changes in purchasing behavior  
       Changes in income  
•       DrugStop:  
o      Profit model:  
       Revenues (price and quantity)  
       Costs  
•       Other/Macro  
o      Government, regulations & taxes  
o      Economic trends

No calculations required  
   
Recommended Conclusion  
DrugStop’s product mix has shifted from being based on high-margin/price drugs to generic 
drugs and convenience store items, both of which are lower-margin/price. This explains its 
decline in profit while increasing market share.  
In order to increase profits, recommended solutions may include changes in product mix 
(balance high-margin drugs with generics), bundling of higher margin products with low margin 
ones, introduction of complementary services, opening new stores in profitable locations, 
introducing an online and delivery store, and even reducing costs to become more profitable 
(closing down stores, reducing personnel, sourcing, etc.).
