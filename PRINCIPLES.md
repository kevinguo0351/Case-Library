# First Principles + The Most-Rewarding Moves

*中文版见 [PRINCIPLES.zh.md](PRINCIPLES.zh.md) · companion to [PATTERNS.md](PATTERNS.md)*

[PATTERNS.md](PATTERNS.md) is the *what* (the surface heuristics). This file is the *why* — the
small set of laws that **generate** all those heuristics, plus, for each case type, the **one bucket
worth leading with** and the **one line worth saying**. If you internalize Part I, you can re-derive
the rest of the playbook at the table without memorizing it.

## Part I — The 10 first-principles laws

Every rewarded move in this library is a corollary of one of these.

### 1. Every case is one identity with a single broken or unknown term.
Almost all 2,141 cases reduce to one of four identities. Your job is to **write the identity, then hunt the term that moved (diagnostic) or is unknown (decision).** Everything else is decoration.
- **Profit = (Price − Variable Cost) × Quantity − Fixed Cost**
- **Value = Σ Cashflowₜ /(1+r)ᵗ**  (shortcut: `FCF/(r−g)`)
- **Market = Population × penetration × frequency × unit-size**  *or*  **installed base ÷ life + new demand**
- **Revenue = Σ over segments (priceᵢ × volumeᵢ)**

*Why it shows in the data:* margin/profit and per-unit math dominate **every** type — because every type is one of these identities being decomposed.

### 2. Name the archetype: Diagnostic vs. Decision.
- **Diagnostic** ("why is X down / what's wrong") → **isolate the driver**: decompose the identity, compare each term to a benchmark, the term that diverged is the answer.
- **Decision** ("should we / which / how much") → there is **always an objective-with-a-number and ≥2 options**; quantify each option against the objective *and a hurdle*, then commit.
Knowing which you're in determines your entire approach. Most candidates blur the two and wander.

### 3. "Compared to what?" — a number alone carries no information.
Insight is **always a comparison**: vs. last year, vs. a competitor, vs. another segment, vs. the target. This is the mandatory first move on any datum or exhibit. ("Costs match the rival but profit is 4× lower → the lever is price" is just this law applied.)

### 4. Averages lie — disaggregate before you analyze.
The answer hides in **one sub-component**; the aggregate conceals it. Ask for the breakdown (by product / segment / region / cost line / cohort) *before* doing any math. This single law is the most-repeated piece of guidance in the corpus and the highest-lift profitability move (decompose price×volume, **1.72×**).

### 5. The objective is a number, and it is the North Star.
Every decision case has a target. **Convert the goal to a figure in the first 60 seconds**, keep it visible, and judge every option against it. Then **close the loop**: "does this hit the target, and by how much?" Weak answers list options; strong answers score them against the number.

### 6. Only incremental, forward-looking cash flows matter.
**Ignore sunk costs entirely; ignore truly fixed costs in marginal decisions.** Say so out loud — it's a deliberate test in many keys. The decision turns only on what *changes* because of the choice.

### 7. Quantify to decide, not to compute.
Math is rewarded when it **kills a hypothesis or flips the recommendation** — not for its own sake. Before a calculation ask: "will the answer change my decision?" If no, skip it. (This is why ~88% of cases have math, yet the prized move is using it to *eliminate*, e.g. "COGS % is flat across 3 years → it's not the cause.")

### 8. Structure is a hypothesis, not a checklist.
A framework should be MECE **and tailored to this case**, and it should encode *where you think the answer is*. The reward is for **driving toward the answer**, not covering all boxes evenly. A generic 4-box template recited without a point of view is explicitly penalized.

### 9. Floor → Anchor → Ceiling, for any "what is it worth / what should it cost / price."
**Cost = floor, competitor/comps = anchor, value-to-the-other-side = ceiling.** Set the number between floor and ceiling. This generalizes far beyond pricing: M&A valuation (standalone floor, comps anchor, strategic-value ceiling), bidding, salary, any negotiation-shaped number.

### 10. Take a stand, then bound it — and follow conservation.
- **Commit** to a recommendation under uncertainty, then name the **one risk that would break it** and the **next step that de-risks it.** Hedging is penalized; "decide + caveat" is rewarded.
- **Conservation ("where did it go?"):** customers, revenue, and share don't vanish. Lost revenue went to a competitor, a substitute, or a shrinking category — decompose the *destination*. A rival who "tripled the market" either took your customers **or** grew the category; the two imply opposite responses.

> **Bonus law — the binding constraint governs.** When something is scarce (capacity, capital, time), value is measured **per unit of that constraint**; find the bottleneck (the minimum link in the chain) and optimize there, not at nameplate.

## Part II — The single most-rewarding bucket per type (lead with this)

For each type: the bucket that (a) usually contains the answer and (b) most candidates under-weight — plus the data that proves it and the one line to open with.

### Profitability → **the disaggregation, not "revenue" or "cost"**
The rewarded move isn't choosing revenue-side or cost-side; it's **breaking the P&L into sub-units to find the one pocket that's broken.** (Clarify competitors 54% / cost-structure 53% / **customer-segment 44%**; the highest-lift action is decomposing into price×volume/segments.)
> *"Before I build the full tree, can I get profit broken out by product / segment / region? Declines are almost always concentrated in one pocket — I'd rather isolate it than average over it."*

### Market Entry → **Right-to-Win, not market attractiveness**
Everyone sizes the market and calls it attractive. The differentiator is **whether *we* can win, tested against the financial hurdle** (capabilities/fit, **lift 1.24×**).
> *"A big, growing market is necessary but not sufficient — I want to test whether we have a genuine right to win here, and whether the entry economics clear our hurdle."*

### Investment / PE → **the financial case bound to a decision rule**
NPV is the dominant math here (**40%**, highest of any type). The reward is **getting to NPV/payback vs. an explicit hurdle** — after finding the value-chain bottleneck so you don't size revenue off nameplate capacity.
> *"Ultimately this is a returns decision, so I'll drive to an NPV/payback against your hurdle rate — but first let me find the binding constraint in the value chain so my revenue figure is real."*

### Growth → **back-calculate the required rate, then test feasibility**
Growth is the most brainstorm-tempting type, but the rewarded move is to **translate the target into a required CAGR/share and pressure-test it** before listing levers, then split organic vs. inorganic on unit economics. (Customer-segment clarify **65%**, the highest of any type.)
> *"Let me first turn the target into a required CAGR and check whether the market can carry it organically, or whether we need to take share / go inorganic."*

### M&A → **price discipline (standalone + synergies vs. the ask)**
The crux bucket is **valuation against price**: standalone value + **separately-quantified** synergies, compared to what you'd pay (synergy/fit **lift 1.56×**; margin & NPV math dominate).
> *"This only creates value if standalone value plus the synergies we can actually capture exceeds the price — so I'll quantify cost synergies (more certain) and revenue synergies (less) separately, then compare to the ask."*

### Pricing → **the value-based ceiling**
Cost-plus is what everyone does; the rewarded lever is **value to the customer, quantified in their currency** (per-unit math **54%**; clarify competitors **62%**). Run all three lenses but anchor on value.
> *"I'll set a cost floor and a competitor anchor, but the real lever is value-based — let me quantify what this is worth to the customer and capture a share of it."*

### Market Sizing → **the equation first, and a verdict at the end**
The rewarded move is **stating the structure before any arithmetic**, then **using the number to answer the real question** (sizing math **64%** here — the structure *is* the answer).
> *"Let me lay out the equation before any numbers — [population → cohort → penetration → frequency → unit] — round as I go, state assumptions, and at the end use the result to answer [the real question]."*

### Operations → **walk the chain to the bottleneck, normalized per-unit**
The reward is **decomposing the process stage by stage to the binding bottleneck**, and **normalizing cost to per-unit** so you can benchmark and spot the outlier stage.
> *"Let me walk the process stage by stage to find the bottleneck, and put cost on a per-unit basis so I can compare to benchmark and see which stage is the real outlier."*

## Part III — Money lines: the most-rewarding thing to say at each moment

The exact sentence that earns points — and *why* it's rewarded (which law it applies).

| Moment | Say this | Law |
|---|---|---|
| **Right after the prompt** | "So the objective is [restate + the number]. Can I take 30 seconds to structure?" | #5 objective; #2 archetype |
| **Asked for your framework** | "Here are [3–5] buckets, and my hypothesis is the answer is in [bucket], because…" | #8 structure-as-hypothesis |
| **Revenue is down** | "Is that price, volume, or mix — and is it concentrated in a segment? Could I see the breakdown?" | #1 identity; #4 disaggregate |
| **Profit down, revenue flat** | "Then it's cost-side — which cost line moved as a % of revenue, and is it fixed or variable?" | #1; #3 compare-to-what |
| **Handed an exhibit** | "What stands out is [outlier] versus [benchmark]; the implication is [so-what]." | #3 compare; #7 quantify-to-decide |
| **A competitor is mentioned** | "Is this share loss to them, a category shift, or both — and what's their cost/price position vs. ours?" | #10 conservation |
| **Asked to brainstorm** | "Let me bucket this into [~4 MECE groups] first, then go deep on the most promising." | #8; PATTERNS §6 |
| **"What should we pay / charge / it's worth?"** | "Cost sets the floor, competitors the anchor, and value to the customer the ceiling — I'll work all three." | #9 floor-anchor-ceiling |
| **"Should we invest / build?"** | "What's the hurdle rate and time horizon? I'll get to an NPV/payback — after I check the binding constraint." | #1 value identity; bonus constraint |
| **Any acquisition** | "First — why this target, why now? Then: does standalone value + capturable synergies beat the price?" | M&A bucket; #9 |
| **Market sizing** | "Let me write the equation first, round aggressively, and flag my key assumption." | sizing bucket; #8 |
| **A sunk/already-spent cost appears** | "That cost is sunk, so I'll exclude it from the forward decision." | #6 incremental-only |
| **Before a big calc** | "Let me check this actually changes the recommendation before I run it." | #7 quantify-to-decide |
| **You get a messy/stuck moment** | "Let me step back to the objective — we're trying to [X]; the question that most moves that is [Y]." | #5; #2 |
| **After any number** | "Sanity-check: that's [≈ a relatable magnitude], which feels [right / off because…]." | #3; recovers errors gracefully |
| **Capacity / scarcity in play** | "Since [resource] is the constraint, I'll rank options by profit per [unit of constraint], not per unit." | bonus constraint law |
| **"Anything else?" / you've solved it** | "Two things I'd also pressure-test: [the unprompted option — divest / partner / license] and [the assumption that would break this]." | #8 driving; #10 stand+bound |
| **The close (always)** | "My recommendation is [X] because [the number]. The biggest risk is [Y]. My next step would be [Z]." | #10 stand+bound; PATTERNS §7 |

### The whole playbook in one breath
**Write the identity → name diagnostic vs. decision → disaggregate and compare to a benchmark → quantify only what changes the answer → judge against the objective → commit, then bound the risk.** Everything in PATTERNS.md is a special case of that.
